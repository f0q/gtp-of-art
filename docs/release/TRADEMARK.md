# Name clearance memo: "GTP of Art"

Prepared 16 Oct 2026 for the owner of **GTP of Art** (Guess The Price of Art), bundle id
`pro.aijam.gtpofart`. Searches were run on 16 Oct 2026.

> **Not legal advice.** This is a desk search by a non-lawyer, made to decide whether to spend money
> on a lawyer. It is not a clearance opinion. Registers lag (UK and EU decisions below are not yet
> reflected in the public records), common-law (unregistered) rights are not covered, and only the US,
> EU and UK were searched. Before filing a trade mark, or if any letter arrives from OpenAI, talk to a
> trade mark attorney.

## 1. Recommendation

**Proceed with "GTP of Art", using the metadata strategy in section 7.** No registered or pending
"GTP OF ART" mark exists in the US, EU or UK, and no app of that name is on the App Store. The legal
risk from OpenAI is low, because "GPT" has been refused or invalidated as descriptive/generic in all
three jurisdictions and the goods differ (an offline art trivia game vs AI language software). The real
costs are practical: App Store search treats "GTP" as "GPT" (section 5), and OpenAI actively polices
names that contain "GPT", so any metadata that leans on the pun invites a complaint. Keep **Going
Once** as the fallback (section 8) in case Apple or OpenAI objects before launch.

## 2. What the name is

"GTP" is an initialism of *Guess The Price*; the owner chose it partly as a play on "GPT" (plan,
Part H, 12 Oct 2026). The letters are transposed, not identical. The in-app rule already in force
(`App/Core/AppInfo.swift`): always the full wordmark "GTP of Art", never "GTP" alone.

That the pun is intentional matters: in a US infringement case, intent to evoke another mark is one
factor courts weigh. It is the main reason to keep every public-facing use away from AI and OpenAI
references (section 7).

The USPTO status of "GPT" matches the published rulings: the USPTO refused GPT as merely descriptive in
2023 and 2024 and, in May 2025, as generic ([Gerben Law](https://www.gerbenlaw.com/?p=70995);

the recognised acronym for "generative pre-trained transformer"; OpenAI's acquired-distinctiveness
claim was not at issue ([Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/chat-gpt-and-2e-the-ttab-rejects-chatgpts-inherent-distinctiveness-case/)).

## 4. OpenAI's position and behaviour

- **Brand guidelines** ([openai.com/brand](https://openai.com/brand/), read 16 Oct 2026): "the 'ChatGPT'
  and 'GPT' brands … are property of OpenAI", and "we do not permit our GPT brand to be used in app,
  product, developer or company names". Model names in app titles are not permitted because "it
  confuses end users. It also triggers our enforcement mechanisms."
  These guidelines govern use of **"GPT"**. "GTP of Art" does not contain "GPT", and the app does not
  use OpenAI's APIs. The guidelines do show that OpenAI watches app names, so the risk is a complaint,
  not a lawsuit.
- **Enforcement record:** as of May 2025 OpenAI had opposed more than 60 GPT-formative US applications
  ([Gerben Law](https://www.gerbenlaw.com/?p=70995)), and in Jan 2026 opposed FREEDOMGPT (Opposition
  91304844), which counter-claimed to cancel OpenAI's GPT-3/GPT-4 registrations
  ([AccessNewswire, FreedomGPT press release](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/computers-technology-and-internet/freedomgpt-files-lawsuit-to-cancel-openais-gpt-trademark-claim-1173760)).
  I found **no** OpenAI opposition or USPTO record against a "GTP" (transposed) mark.
- **Likelihood of confusion with GPT marks: low.** The goods differ (art trivia game vs AI software);
  "GTP" is a different letter string that stands for something else; "of Art" and the subtitle
  "Guess the Price of Art" spell out the meaning; and OpenAI's "GPT" is refused (US), invalidated
  pending finality (EU), and reportedly invalidated (UK). The remaining risk factor is the intentional
  pun: avoid any AI or OpenAI references in the name, icon, screenshots or keywords.
- Do **not** file a US/EU/UK application for "GTP OF ART" at launch. A filing is the event OpenAI's
  watch service sees and opposes, and an opposition would cost more than the app earns. Revisit if
  the app grows.

## 5. App Store search findings

Checked with Apple's iTunes Search API (the public interface behind App Store search), US and GB
storefronts, 16 Oct 2026:

- **"GTP"**: every top result is an AI chat app (ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, DeepSeek, …). App Store search
  treats "GTP" as a misspelling of "GPT". No app titled "GTP" ranks; the only "GTP"-named app found by
  web search is GTP Toolbox, an industrial utility
  ([apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/np/app/gtp-toolbox/id1565077431)). The "ChatGTP" lookalike
  that topped a Hong Kong chart in 2023 was flagged as a copycat ([HK01](https://www.hk01.com/sns/article/60226258)).
- **"GTP of Art"**: no app with that name; results are general art apps (Artsy, Google Arts & Culture,
  …). The name is very likely free in App Store Connect (names must be unique; only App Store Connect
  can confirm).
- **"Guess the price"**: Guess The Price (Justin McArdle, 0 ratings), HousePricer, **The Right Price Is?**
  (Imran Gul, ~227 ratings, has an art category), Housle, The Correct Price, and "The Price Is Right:
  Bingo!" (licensed). Art-specific: **Which painting is expensive?** (11 ratings)
  ([apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/which-painting-is-expensive/id579876624),
  [apps.apple.com](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/the-right-price-is/id1559178579)). None uses "GTP".

**The search problem.** Someone who hears "GTP of Art" and types "gtp" will see ChatGPT and other
chatbots, not this app, until the app has enough installs for Apple to match the exact title. Typing
"gtp of art" should match the title directly once the app is live (verify on launch day). Mitigations:
the subtitle "Guess the Price of Art" puts *guess*, *price* and *art* in the indexed name fields;
share cards and social posts should say "search **Guess the Price of Art**", not "search GTP"; and the
App Store link (or QR code) belongs on every share card and post.

## 6. App Review guidelines

Quotes from the [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/):

- **4.1 Copycats.** (a) don't "make some minor changes to another app's name … and pass it off as your
  own"; (c) "You cannot use another developer's icon, brand, or product name in your app's icon or
  name". **Low risk:** "GTP of Art" is not another app's name, the icon candidates (gilt monogram, frame, gavel) and the UI
  look nothing like ChatGPT, and the app does nothing AI-related. Risk rises only if the metadata,
  icon or screenshots evoke ChatGPT (chat bubbles, the OpenAI knot, "AI" wording).
- **5.2.1 Intellectual property.** No "misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or
  metadata". Same mitigation.
- **2.3.7 Accurate metadata.** Don't "pack any of your metadata with trademarked terms, popular app
  names"; subtitles must not "reference other apps". So **never put "GPT", "ChatGPT", "AI",
  "Christie's" or "Sotheby's" in the keywords, subtitle or description.**
- **5.2.5 Apple products.** About apps "confusingly similar to an existing Apple product, interface …
  app … or advertising theme". **Not triggered**: the name contains no Apple marks, and the app
  does not imitate an Apple app, interface or ad campaign.
- If OpenAI complains, it would go through Apple's content dispute process, and Apple would ask the
  developer to respond or rename. That is the realistic worst case: a forced rename after launch.
  Keeping the fallback ready (section 8) caps the cost.

## 7. Required metadata strategy if proceeding

1. App name exactly **GTP of Art**; subtitle **Guess the Price of Art** (spells out the initialism).
2. First line of the description explains the name: "GTP of Art stands for Guess The Price of Art."
3. No "GPT", "ChatGPT", "AI" or "OpenAI" anywhere in App Store metadata, screenshots, the icon or
   the keyword field. Don't promote the pun in store copy; if the owner wants it, keep it to personal
   social posts and keep it light ("no AI, just you and the art").
4. Keywords target what players type: auction, painting, quiz, trivia, masterpiece, … (see
   `APP_STORE.md`).
5. Never use "GTP" alone as a brand (already a code rule).
6. Register `gtpofart.com` now (it was unregistered on 16 Oct 2026) so the web presence matches the
   name, and point it at the `gtpofart.aijam.pro` pages.
